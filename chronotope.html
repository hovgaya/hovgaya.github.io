How to read the map

The map moves in steps: one decade every 3 seconds at ×1. ■ Stop (or Esc) freezes the map so you can zoom in and open points; ◀ 10 and 10 ▶ (or the arrow keys) step one decade back or forward; the space bar plays and pauses. Click a point to see the name string at this place: every recorded form there, each on its own time bar. When a new name replaces an older one at the same place, its label reads "old → new".

Each point is one recorded name form at one place. It fades in over 20 years before its first record, stays while it is recorded, and fades out over 40 years after its last record. Points grow with the number of names recorded together at the same place: a centre with many names is a centre with a long and many-voiced record. Only Armenian-language name forms are shown. Records of the same places in other languages (Greek, Latin, Akkadian, Persian, Ottoman and modern Turkish, English and others) are not part of this map; they are kept for reference in a separate file. Grey points before AD 405 are places on record whose Armenian name is known only from later sources.

Territory. The map covers the Armenian Highland, Lesser Armenia/Sophene and Cilicia. Places of neighbouring lands that fall inside these frames are left out: Kartli and eastern Georgia beyond Javakhk and Lori-Tashir (e.g. Tbilisi), the lands north of the Kura, and Atropatene south of the Araxes (e.g. Tabriz, Ardabil). The 100 excluded records are listed in a separate file, so the exclusion can be checked and revised.

Dates are as precise as their sources: Pleiades assigns many names to broad periods (e.g. "Roman", 30 BC – AD 300), and sometimes extends a modern name over the whole life of a site; such names are not shown before the earliest record of their language (e.g. Turkish forms not before 1900, Ottoman before 1300, Arabic before 630, Armenian before 405); Wikidata foundation dates mark the first record of a place; before AD 405 such places are drawn in grey as sites on record, with the modern Armenian label for orientation only. This is a prototype for verification, kept separate from the epigraphic datasets of the article.
